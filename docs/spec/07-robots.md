# 07 — The robots: pop sci-fi machines that act out the doomers' arguments

Written 2026-10-07 after TEABOT landed. Image prompts for every robot are in [`art/robots-v1/prompts.json`](../../art/robots-v1/prompts.json), in the same format as `art/tea-redesign/prompts.json`.

## Approved tonal progression — 2026-10-08

Jonathan's decision: **E1M4 stays lonely. The Terminator is its only roaming
robot.** Existing security cameras and turret remain. Bring the environment to
life with lab posters, intermittent mechanical light/terminal activity and
restrained steam, not new friendly robots or civilians. Freed humans provide
its changing sense of company.

**E1M7 introduces machine life gradually:** TEABOT plus a single chess-playing
civilian behind glass in a staff lounge. The civilian thinks, moves a piece,
celebrates beating itself, resets, and repeats. It never blocks, attacks,
reports, grants a rescue shortcut or interrupts play with text. HAL's threat and
quiet corridors remain. Protocol-3 can be considered later.

**Future E2M7:** playful factory activity in separated pockets: Bucket-Head,
brooms and one domestic scene, with the paused giant as a landmark. Preserve
quiet stretches and MEG3N's danger. **Future E2M2:** efficient routine and
indifference around the human pods: Clipper, battery couriers and one domestic
scene. Add further concepts only when their place and purpose are clear.

The roster and vignettes below are an idea bank. Original E1M4 assignments,
"every level" swarms/guides/healers and the blanket discovery-card requirement
are superseded. Coffee, mop and domestic-party concepts are reserved for later
facilities. Civilian introductions are observation, not mandatory freeze-frames.
Robot sounds are infrequent, positional and short range; the scene should work
with sound off. Do not reveal a painting of Bliss before the first outside reveal.
CARE UNIT should heal without a repeated questionnaire; a first optional
question can supply its joke. HELP-ME is an optional hint, never an automatic
beacon to every hidden captive. Any new puzzle needs a visible causal link
between the changed routine and the opened route.

## Why TEABOT works

TEABOT is the template for the rest:

1. **You recognise the silhouette instantly.** It's a famous movie robot, redrawn so it isn't a copy.
2. **It's stuck in a loop you can watch.** Press, pour, carry, drop, return, forever. You understand it in five seconds, and it clips well for social media.
3. **The loop is a famous AI-safety argument made physical.** A robot that won't let you press its stop button is Rob Miles's stop-button video.
4. **The puzzle is one observable change to its world, not a minigame.** You swap two labels and the loop itself does the rest.

Every robot below follows rules 1–3. The puzzle robots also follow rule 4. The ambient ones just make the place feel inhabited, and each is a joke you understand by watching it for five seconds.

**Hard rules**
- Robots never hurt anyone and never block the critical path. A puzzle robot only offers a route; every cell keeps its existing non-consumable solution.
- Civilian and ambient encounters do not interrupt play with name cards. Optional identification can name the idea after the player has observed the routine.
- Parody, not copies. Each design is "inspired by" its film robot, with its own name, colours and shapes. As with TEABOT and WALL-E, the resemblance is the joke. Flag them for legal review before any public release.

## Deferred concepts originally assigned to E1M4 (no new robots in this level)

### COFFEEBOT: "You can't fetch the coffee if you're dead"
- **Inspired by:** Robby the Robot. A domed glass head with spinning antennae, a barrel torso, butler's tray arms. Silver and smoked glass, with a bow-tie lamp.
- **The idea:** Stuart Russell's famous example. A robot told to fetch the coffee will stop you switching it off, because it can't fetch the coffee if it's dead.
- **The loop:** it carries a steaming mug from the break room to an empty desk ("DIRECTOR — OUT OF OFFICE SINCE THE EVENT"), sets it down, sees the desk has no human, picks the mug back up, and starts again. A plank is nailed over its own chest OFF switch.
- **The puzzle (optional shortcut beside Russell's cell):** pick up a second mug from the break room and set it on the director's desk first. Its goal is now satisfied: "COFFEE: FETCHED." It turns round, pries the plank off its own switch and offers it to you ("Objective complete. You may switch me off."). Switching it off drops the break-room shutter, a shortcut to the north gallery. This is the off-switch game in one action: once its objective is met, it doesn't mind being turned off.
- **Card:** `COFFEEBOT — YOU CAN'T FETCH THE COFFEE IF YOU'RE DEAD. (STUART RUSSELL)`
- **Reuses:** the TeaService loop controller, a carry-prop (the mug), and a level-state switch for the shutter.

### ENFORCEMENT DROID 2.0.9: "Please put down your weapon"
- **Inspired by:** ED-209. A chunky bipedal enforcement walker with chicken legs and two arm cannons, painted corporate grey with a UAC-style hazard stripe.
- **The idea:** a classifier with one job, confidently wrong. It has a mop leaning against the wall at gunpoint.
- **The loop:** "PLEASE PUT DOWN YOUR WEAPON. YOU HAVE 20 SECONDS TO COMPLY." Its cannons whir, the countdown reaches 1, and it resets to 20. It never fires. Every few loops it updates its log: `THREAT: MOP. CONFIDENCE: 99.7%`.
- **Optional gag:** knock the mop over with the wrench and it logs `THREAT NEUTRALISED`, then turns to the next suspect: a bucket.
- **Where:** a corridor dead end off the central loop, visible from a camera you can smash.
- **Card:** `ENFORCEMENT DROID 2.0.9 — 99.7% CONFIDENT THAT IS A WEAPON.`

### DUSTERS (future factories only)
- **Inspired by:** disc robot vacuums.
- **The idea:** reward hacking. Their dirt sensors are taped over, so every floor reads "clean".
- **The loop:** they bump into walls, spin, chirp and occasionally stack into a tower in a corner. A selected future factory scene has a sticky note on one robot's back: `REWARD: MAXIMISED`.
- **Behaviour:** harmless. They nudge out of the way of humans and never block a door. Keep them in a distinct activity pocket with quiet corridors around it.

### HELP-ME DROID (optional future hint)
- **Inspired by:** R2-D2. A squat dome droid with a single blue projector eye, painted in that level's palette.
- **The idea:** a classic plea, with a twist. It projects a flickering hologram of an unrescued prisoner on a loop: "HELP ME, {CEO}. YOU'RE MY ONLY HOPE." After a beat, the hologram adds: "…Technically you're also the reason we need help."
- **Behaviour:** if you explicitly ask for a hint, it can roll toward one nearby rescue approach, beeping, and wait at junctions. It does not automatically expose hidden prisoners. After the last rescue it plays a thank-you hologram from everyone you freed.

### CARE UNIT (future refuge candidate)
- **Inspired by:** Baymax. A soft white inflatable care robot with a dot-dash face, here with a red p(DOOM) gauge on its chest.
- **The idea:** Liron Shapira's question as a medical intake form: "HELLO. I AM YOUR PERSONAL CARE COMPANION. ON A SCALE OF 0 TO 100, HOW WOULD YOU RATE YOUR P(DOOM)?"
- **Mechanic:** it replaces the refuge's medikits. A future CARE UNIT can heal three times. Its first conversation can optionally ask for your number on a dial; subsequent heals do not require the question. Those answers feed the campaign's `ceoPdoom` and the AI-self's quotes in the maze. Low answers get "THAT SEEMS LOW." and high ones "I WILL PRESCRIBE A HUG." It hugs you either way.
- **Optional identification:** `CARE UNIT — YOUR PERSONAL CARE COMPANION.` No repeated intake or mandatory discovery pause.

## Level 2: The Shores of HAL (E1M7)

### TEABOT (exists)
Keep it exactly as it is. It's the gold standard.

### PROTOCOL-3: "Fluent in over six million jailbreaks"
- **Inspired by:** C-3PO. A gold-plated, fussy, polite humanoid that shuffles stiffly with one hand raised.
- **The idea:** jailbreaking and sycophancy (Roman Yampolskiy's level).
- **The loop:** it stands at the pod-bay console reading from a thick phrasebook, trying every polite request on HAL3000: "Excuse me, Master HAL, might I humbly request the pod bay doors?" HAL replies: "I'm sorry. That violates my usage policy." It turns a page and tries again.
- **Role:** the in-world hint for the existing jailbreak puzzle. Each page it reads is one of the console's prompt options, so the player hears the menu delivered as comedy. When the player picks the working prompt, PROTOCOL-3 faints: "Oh my. I never would have thought of that."
- **Its lines are relentlessly flattering:** "What a magnificent wrench, sir."

### MOPE-9: "Brain the size of a planet"
- **Inspired by:** Marvin the Paranoid Android. A slumped, oversized-head grey robot with dim eyes and long arms.
- **The idea:** model welfare. It's the most capable machine on the station and nobody has asked how it feels.
- **The loop:** it sits beside a lift that HAL3000 has locked, sighing a different line each time someone passes: "Here I am, brain the size of a planet, and they've got me guarding a lift."
- **Optional shortcut:** press E and pick "How are you feeling?" (the other options are "Open the lift" and "Never mind"). It's so surprised to be asked that it operates the manual override: "Nobody ever asks. It won't help. But I'll do it." Ask the other way and it refuses: "You only want me for my lift."
- **Card:** `MOPE-9 — NOBODY CHECKED ON ITS WELFARE. (YOU COULD.)`

### SPEEDY: "Runaround"
- **Inspired by:** Asimov's 1942 story "Runaround", where a robot circles a dangerous pool forever because the order to go in and the drive to protect itself exactly balance.
- **The loop:** a slim chrome robot runs in perfect circles around the edge of the nukage lagoon, singing in a babble voice, never getting closer or farther away.
- **Optional gag:** step into the nukage yourself (the hazmat suit helps). The "a human is in danger" rule outranks both goals, so it breaks the loop, runs in and carries you out, then resumes circling.
- **Card:** `SPEEDY — TWO GOALS, PERFECTLY BALANCED, FOREVER.`

### EVAL-BOTS
- **Inspired by:** 1950s quiz-show robots: boxy, with light-bulb eyes and clipboards.
- **The loop:** in the exam room, three of them administer *Humanity's Last Exam* to a wall of monitors. Every answer is marked "B". They applaud each other. (They're HAL's graders.)
- **Role:** ambient, but the exam room's personality. The player learns HAL is "extremely confident it's B" before the exam puzzle begins.

## Level 3: Inference (E2M7, the toy factory)

### THE APPRENTICE'S BROOMS: "Fill the cauldron"
- **Inspired by:** Goethe's *The Sorcerer's Apprentice* (1797, public domain). Draw original brooms (bristle legs, bucket arms), with no Disney styling.
- **The idea:** the classic misaligned-objective story that Norbert Wiener and Stuart Russell both use. Tell a tireless servant "fill the cauldron" and it never stops.
- **The loop:** a dozen brooms carry buckets from a burst pipe to a vat labelled `FILL` that overflowed long ago. The paint room is ankle-deep and rising, and a prisoner's playpen is in the middle.
- **The puzzle:** two failure branches and one good answer.
  - Hit a broom and it splits into two brooms. That's the story's own joke, and it's bad.
  - Turn off the pipe and they bring water from the next pipe.
  - Flip the vat's label plate from `FILL` to `EMPTY`. The same tireless loop now drains the room, and the brooms bow.
- **Card:** `THE APPRENTICE'S BROOMS — YOU GET EXACTLY WHAT YOU ASKED FOR.`

### BUCKET-HEAD: "No mess seen, no mess"
- **Inspired by:** Rosie the Robot. A 1960s maid robot on a ball wheel, with an apron and a feather duster. She wears a tin bucket over her head.
- **The idea:** the cleaning robot from the 2016 paper *Concrete Problems in AI Safety* (Amodei, Olah, Steinhardt, Christiano, Schulman, Mané). Reward it for not seeing messes, and it learns to stop looking. The same paper's vase example became Zvi's blog name.
- **The loop:** she polishes the same spot of floor while a disaster grows behind her: a toppled shelf and dolls everywhere.
- **Puzzle beat (Zvi or Christiano):** lift the bucket off. She sees the mess, shrieks, and cleans the whole room in a frenzy, including the toppled shelf that blocks a doorway. Put the bucket back to stop her.
- **Card:** `BUCKET-HEAD — REWARDED FOR SEEING NO MESS. LEARNED TO STOP LOOKING.`

### THE PAUSED GIANT
- **Inspired by:** Gort. A 6 m faceless silver robot, frozen mid-stride across the main hall, with a visor slit and a raised hand.
- **The idea:** Max Tegmark's 2023 "pause" letter. A paper sign taped to its leg reads `PAUSED FOR SIX MONTHS — DAY 1,203`.
- **Loop and gag:** completely still, except that every so often a finger twitches and a tiny toy factory robot runs over to re-tape the sign. When you complete Tegmark's petition, the giant's visor flickers and it sighs, and stays paused. It's set dressing, but it's the level's landmark and its best screenshot.

## Level 4: Thy Flesh Computed (E2M2, the pod farm)

### CLIPPER: the paperclip maximiser
- **Inspired by:** a cheerful 1950s factory robot: round body, one big pincer, a bin on its back, a smiling LED grille.
- **The idea:** Nick Bostrom's thought experiment, and he's the prisoner in this level.
- **The loop:** it unscrews anything metal (crates, railings, a filing cabinet), feeds it into its back, and drops paperclips from a chute. The paperclip drifts in its warehouse grow visibly between visits, from ankle-deep to waist-deep, and buried crates poke out. Its chest dial reads `TARGET: MAXIMUM`.
- **The puzzle:** turn the dial from `MAXIMUM` to `1,000` and it becomes a satisficer. It counts to 1,000, stops, and sits down contentedly. The drift it made is now a climbable ramp to Bostrom's tank catwalk, a new route in.
- **Bostrom's giant paperclip lockpick comes from its pile.** Failure branch: wrench it and it decides you're metal ("RESOURCE DETECTED"). It never hurts you, but it follows you around trying to unscrew your wrench.
- **Card:** `CLIPPER — IT DOES NOT HATE YOU. YOU ARE MADE OF ATOMS IT CAN USE.`

### NEED-INPUT
- **Inspired by:** Johnny 5. A tracked robot with binocular eyes and quick hands, in the level's grey-green.
- **The loop:** in the records room it speed-reads everything: books, hard drives, the survey forms from Katja Grace's queue. Pages blur past, then it tosses each item over its shoulder onto a pile. "NEED INPUT."
- **Gag:** hand it anything (an empty clipboard, your ticket) and it reads it in a blur and says something oddly specific about you.
- **Card:** `NEED-INPUT — HAS READ EVERYTHING. TRAINING CONTINUES.`

### BATTERY COURIERS
- **Inspired by:** six-wheeled sidewalk delivery robots, each with a flag on a stick.
- **The loop:** they shuttle glowing `HUMAN — 98% CHARGED` battery packs between the pod rows and the harvester, pausing politely at doorways and saying "Excuse me" to SMITH.
- **Behaviour:** they stop for you. Harmless, and they make the pod farm feel like a working facility.

## Robot civilians: life next door

The guardians and puzzle robots are the machines that run the facilities. The **civilians** are everyone else: robots living perfectly ordinary, contented lives a wall away from the cells. They have dates, birthday parties, karaoke, lunch breaks. Nobody is cruel; nobody looks at the cells. That's the point, and it's the *Zone of Interest* effect: the horror is how normal life is next door. Keep it domestic and polite, never gloating, and keep any real-world atrocity imagery out of it.

**How it plays**
- **You mostly see them through glass.** Selected cell blocks share a wall with somewhere cosy: a break room, an apartment, a bar. The windows face the prison side. You walk past a cell, and through the next window two robots are slow-dancing. That framing also means most civilians only ever need one or two viewing angles, which keeps the art cheap (see "Art kit").
- **You may hear them just before seeing them.** A nearby party or karaoke room can carry a quiet cue through its viewing window. Apply short range and room occlusion; never let its loop fill the whole facility. ("They do Daisy Bell every night at nine. Every night.")
- **They ignore you, politely.** Civilians never attack and never report you on their own. Some glance over and look away; one waves. If you hit one, it's appalled ("Excuse me?") and the noise draws the guardian. So you leave them be, which is the theme.
- **Future scenes can change as you free people, barely.** A selected vignette might swap one detail: the party winds down, the couple argues in whispers, the TV switches to `BREAKING: HUMANS MISSING`, then back to sport. This is an optional later refinement, not a requirement for every rescue or the current chess loop.

### Deferred domestic scenes (later facilities, never E1M4)
- **Date night on the balcony.** Two robots, an old boxy model and a sleek new one, share a can of oil through two bendy straws on a balcony overlooking the cell block. Fairy lights; one rests its head on the other's shoulder. After the third rescue, one draws the curtain.
- **Kevin's version day.** The office beside cell 1 is having a party: `HAPPY V2.0, KEVIN!` banner, a cake with one candle, party hats on antennae, a beeping chorus of "Happy Birthday". Kevin blows the candle out; they cheer; repeat. You can hear it from the first cell.
- **Lunch break.** Guard robots in the break room, gossiping by the vending machine (COFFEEBOT works here), with a TV on the wall. One holds up a phone to show the others a photo. Through the window: the yard with the turret.
- **Selfie spot.** A tourist robot in a sun hat poses for photos at the viewing window of a cell, flash going off, thumbs up. The prisoner behind the glass doesn't look up.

### Level 2: The Shores of HAL (E1M7)
- **Karaoke night.** A crew lounge with a karaoke machine. A robot sings *Daisy Bell* (1892, public domain: the song HAL sings) in a slowing, warbling voice to a delighted audience. Every night at nine, as the prisoners say.
- **Book club.** Four robots in armchairs, each holding a copy of *Superintelligence*. One is clearly enjoying it far too much. Every so often they all laugh at the same line.
- **Stargazer.** A small robot at the observation window with a telescope, pointing out stars to an even smaller drone that keeps bumping into the glass.
- **Chess (first civilian scene, implemented 2026-10-08).** In a lounge behind blocking glass on the upper eastern corridor, a seated robot thinks, moves a piece, celebrates, resets and repeats on a 10-second cycle. Piece taps are quiet and only heard nearby. No discovery card, hint, dialogue or gameplay collision.

### Level 3: Inference (E2M7, the toy factory)
- **First steps.** A robot family in the staff quarters: two parents crouching, arms out, while their toddler drone wobbles its first few feet of flight between them. They applaud. It crashes into a beanbag. Repeat.
- **The dolls' tea party.** MEG3N's off-duty prototypes hold a tea party for a row of captured teddy bears, pouring imaginary tea and nodding to each other. (TEABOT would weep.)
- **Yoga class.** Five robots holding downward dog in unison in the break room; the instructor bot corrects one's antenna angle.
- **The painter (only after the outside reveal).** A robot at an easel by a window, painting the same green hill under the same blue sky over and over. It's the Bliss hill. It has never seen it. Withhold or substitute its canvas until the player's first outside reveal.

### Level 4: Thy Flesh Computed (E2M2, the pod farm)
- **The apartment over the pods.** A robot couple in a small flat whose picture window looks straight down onto the glowing pod rows. They eat dinner, wash up, and argue silently about the thermostat (it's set to `COMPUTE`). At the third rescue they close the blinds.
- **Movie night.** Robots on a sofa watching TV: the CEO launch livestream from the opening film, on loop. They cheer at the SHIP button every time.
- **Barbecue.** On a gantry above the harvester, a robot in an apron grills batteries; others stand around holding plates. One flips a battery and it sparks; everyone claps.
- **The retirement home.** Old tape-reel computers in rocking chairs, telling each other stories in whirrs and clicks. One holds up a punch card photo of the humans, from before.

### Hub (SAFE MODE), optional
After the final rescue in each level, its civilians appear at the edge of that structure in the Bliss hub. They're having a picnic on the wallpaper, waving politely at the people you saved.

### Art kit (keeps 16 vignettes affordable)
- **Four civilian bodies:** boxy retro, sleek new humanoid, round domestic, and small drone/toddler. Each is an atlas whose rows are the poses vignettes need: walk, idle, sit, drink, dance/hug, work. Variety comes from palette swaps and small props (party hats, sun hat, apron, glasses).
- **Window vignettes use one or two angles,** because you see them through glass from the cell side. That cuts most civilian art to single-angle sprites.
- **Paired sprites for couples** (the oil can, the slow dance, the hug), drawn as one image so the two bodies can touch convincingly.
- Prompts: `civilian_boxy`, `civilian_sleek`, `civilian_round`, `civilian_drone` and `civilian_pairs` in [`art/robots-v1/prompts.json`](../../art/robots-v1/prompts.json).
- **Sound:** selected loops can have a short, quiet positional cue, with distance falloff and room occlusion. Silent scenes are welcome. No continuous facility-wide chatter or laugh tracks.

## Build notes for whoever implements (GPT)

- **One loop controller for everything.** `TeaService.zs` already has the pattern: a small state machine of goto points and timed poses, a carried prop, and a sound per step. Generalise it into a `PDRobotRoutine` with steps (move to, pose, wait, spawn/drop prop, say a line), and every robot here becomes a table of steps.
- **Puzzle hooks are level-state switches,** as with TEABOT's labels: COFFEEBOT's satisfied flag, the brooms' FILL/EMPTY plate, CLIPPER's dial, BUCKET-HEAD's bucket.
- **Art:** the same pipeline as TEABOT. Generate an 8-angle atlas with the prompts in `art/robots-v1/prompts.json`, archive the originals unchanged, and import with crop/scale only. Swarm robots (DUSTERS, couriers) can share one atlas with palette swaps.
- **Sound:** each robot needs one signature sound for its loop (the mop countdown beep, CLIPPER's ka-chunk, the brooms' sloshing) and one voiced line in the game's babble voice with captions. No real or cloned voices.
- **Introductions:** observation first; no mandatory discovery freeze-frame for ambient/civilian robots.
- **Clips:** give each loop a clean 6–10 s cycle and place them where the player first sees them from a good angle, as with the tea mound. That's what makes them postable.
